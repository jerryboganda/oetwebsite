Test modes and Speaking
- Paper bookings go to registration.myoet.com and computer bookings to booking.oet-global.com (oet.com venue finder "Book now" links; https://support.oet.com/articles/Knowledge/How-do-I-book-OET; "have your Visa or MasterCard and ID ready")
- Saudi Arabia is one of the countries where OET on Computer candidates can take the Speaking sub-test at a venue instead of at home (https://oet.com/post/speaking-sub-test-available-for-select-countries, dated 20 Jul 2026; "Candidates in the below regions can now take the Speaking sub-test at a venue")
- OET tells candidates to check that the Speaking mode is accepted by their regulator (https://oet.com/test/book-a-test; "please ensure the Speaking mode you select is accepted by the organisation")
- OET@Home: no OET@Home option was listed in the Saudi results (only Computer and Paper listings), and OET says OET@Home exists where venue testing is not available (https://oet.com/test/oet-test-at-home; "offered in countries where taking the test at a venue is not yet available"). OET does not publish a country list, so "OET@Home is not offered in Saudi Arabia" is an inference from these two facts, not a sentence on oet.com.
- Booking deadlines: paper closes 24 days before the test, computer 7 days before (https://oet.com/test/book-a-test; "Bookings for OET Test on Paper close 24 days before the test date")
- Already on site (oet-test-dates-and-fees): OET on Paper covers 7 professions, OET on Computer and OET@Home all 12; computer results in as little as 48 hours, 95% within 6 calendar days; paper results from 13 days.

## 2. Test fee and payment

- The full OET test costs AU$587 in Saudi Arabia, the same for paper, computer and OET@Home (https://support.oet.com/articles/Knowledge/How-much-does-OET-cost; "costs AU$587 (or US$455 for OET on Computer at a venue and OET@Home in the US)")
- The OET@Home page shows the same fee (https://oet.com/test/oet-test-at-home; "AUD $587")
- Payment is by card (https://support.oet.com/articles/Knowledge/How-do-I-book-OET; "be sure to have your Visa or MasterCard and ID ready")
- Single sub-test re-sits: AU$315 / AU$415 / AU$515 for 1 / 2 / 3 sub-tests, OET on Paper only, after a full test (https://support.oet.com/articles/Knowledge/How-much-does-OET-cost; "Here are the prices (including the AU$7 online booking fee)")
- Taxes: the two OET fee pages read today say nothing about VAT, GST, a Saudi price or SAR payment. See "Could not verify".
- Already on site (oet-exam-fees): AU$587 is shown as approximately SAR 1,510, with the note that the bank or card provider sets the real rate and may add a foreign-currency fee. This is the site's own conversion, not an OET price.

## 3. Does SCFHS require an English test?

- No English test is named on the SCFHS classification requirements page. It lists qualifications, verification, registration certificate, experience and an employment letter, and no IELTS, OET or TOEFL (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/professional-classification-requirements; "The verification result issued by the verification company approved by the SCFHS")
- No English test is named on the SCFHS registration requirements page either (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/professional-registration-requirements; "It must be issued within three months prior to submitting the application")
- The SCFHS Mumaris FAQ only sets a language rule for documents (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/mumaris-faq; "the language of the attached certificate must be in Arabic or English only")
- The SMLE, SNLE and SPLE applicant guides read today contain no English test requirement (full text searched for English, IELTS and OET: no match).
- Already on site (gulf-pathway-guide): "the SCFHS classification requirements we checked do not set an OET score"; employers can set their own requirement.
- Safe wording for the page: "The SCFHS pages we checked do not ask for an OET or IELTS score for classification." Do not write "SCFHS never requires English": SCFHS training programmes do set English scores (not checked in detail today), and the Arabic General Bylaws were not read.

## 4. Licensing route (facts only)

- Portal: applications are made in Mumaris+ (Mumaris Plus) (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/mumaris-faq; "You can apply for the Service through the Mumaris+ system")
- Verification: certificates from outside the Kingdom are verified at source through DataFlow (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/practitioner; "by contacting the certificate-issuing body by DataFlow, the international and specialized company")
- The verification report is a classification requirement; Saudis with Ministry of Education equalised foreign qualifications and holders of recognised qualifications from inside the Kingdom are exempt (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/professional-classification-requirements; "The verification result issued by the verification company approved by the SCFHS")
- Other classification documents: qualification details, a valid professional registration certificate, experience (at least one year for non-Saudis, except graduates of Saudi universities), an employment letter issued within three months, and a medical report for applicants over 65 (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/professional-classification-requirements; summarised from the page, no verbatim quote captured for each item).
- Licensing exams: registration for the licensure exams SMLE, SDLE, SNLE, SPLE, SLLE, SRCLE and SRTLE is a Mumaris Plus service, fee SAR 120, 5 business days (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/node/1991; "It is an electronic service for registering in professional practice licensure exams")
- Exam delivery: Prometric (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/classification-exams; "This service allows accessing the Prometric website to book an appointment to take the test")
- Candidates can sit in Saudi Arabia or abroad (https://scfhs.org.sa/sites/default/files/2025-09/SPLE%20Applicant%20Guide.docx_.pdf; "any SCFHS approved Prometric testing center locally and internationally")
- Format, pharmacists (SPLE): 200 questions in two parts of 100, 120 minutes each, 30-minute break (same SPLE guide; "It consists of 200 questions divided into 2 parts")
- Format, doctors (SMLE): 200 MCQs, four hours, two sections of 100 (https://scfhs.org.sa/sites/default/files/2024-06/SMLE%20Applicant%20Guide%202024_0.pdf; "The testing period is 4 hours.")
- Format, nurses (SNLE): 200 MCQs in two parts of 100, 120 minutes each (https://scfhs.org.sa/sites/default/files/2023-03/Saudi%20Nursing%20Lisencure%20Examination%20Applicant%20Guide.pdf; "consists of 200 MCQs, which may include up to 10% of pilot questions")
- Attempts: up to four a year until a pass (SPLE guide; "may take SPLE up to four times a year"; the SMLE and SNLE guides say the same)
- The SCFHS service-level list names both test companies (https://scfhs.org.sa/en/mumaris-faq; "An electronic written exam (Prometric, Pearson VUE) - 30 working days")
- Already on site (gulf-pathway-guide): "Classification through Mumaris+, document verification by an SCFHS-approved company and Saudi licensing exams (the pharmacist exam, SPLE, runs at SCFHS-approved Prometric centres)."

## 5. Why candidates in Saudi Arabia still take OET (regulator facts)

All of these are "already on site" (oet-score-requirements page, verified 2 Nov 2026), not re-fetched today:
- UK nurses and midwives (NMC): OET Nursing 350 in Listening, Reading and Speaking, 300 in Writing; 1 sitting or 2 within 12 months; OET@Home accepted; valid 2 years.
- UK doctors (GMC): OET Medicine 350 (grade B) in all four sub-tests in one sitting, the most recent test; OET@Home accepted; valid 2 years.
- UK pharmacists (GPhC): 350 in all four, one sitting, OET@Home not accepted.
- Ireland nurses (NMBI): 350 / 350 / 300 Writing / 350, one sitting, no combining, OET@Home not accepted.
- Ireland doctors (Medical Council of Ireland): 350 in each module in a single sitting (applications from 1 March 2025), OET@Home not accepted.
- Australia (Ahpra, tests from 23 April 2026): 350 Listening, 360 Reading, 350 Writing, 360 Speaking; 1 sitting or 2 within 12 months; OET@Home not accepted.
- New Zealand: Nursing Council 350 / 350 / 300 / 350; Medical Council 350 / 350 / 300 / 350.
- USA: ECFMG 2027 Pathways 350 / 350 / 300 Writing / 350 in one test taken on or after 1 January 2025; OET@Home only if there is no venue in the candidate's country (Saudi Arabia has venues, see section 1).
- Local point that is verified today: because Saudi Arabia has test venues and at-venue Speaking, a candidate there can sit a fully venue-based test, which matters for the regulators above that do not accept OET@Home.

## 6. Time zone

- Saudi Arabia is UTC+3 all year, with no daylight saving rule (IANA Time Zone Database, release 2026f, file "asia", https://data.iana.org/time-zones/data/asia; "Zone Asia/Riyadh 3:06:52 - LMT 1947 Mar 14" followed by "3:00 - %z")
- OET computer and OET@Home bookings close at 11:59 pm UTC, which is 2:59 am the next day in Saudi Arabia (calculated from the line above and https://oet.com/test/book-a-test; "closes 7 days before the test date (11:59PM UTC time)")
