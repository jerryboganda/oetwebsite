OET clubbing rules 2027: who lets you combine two sittings

Clubbing means using the results of two OET sittings together to meet one score requirement. OET does not decide whether you can do it: your regulator does. Some regulators publish clear two-sitting rules, many accept one sitting only, and the conditions are different almost everywhere.

This guide sets out, regulator by regulator, what each one's own website says about combining sittings: the scores, the floor in every sitting, the time window and the rule on single sub-test retakes. Where a regulator's page is silent, we say "not confirmed" instead of guessing.

By Dr Ahmed Hesham, medical doctor and founder of OET with Dr Hesham · Published 27 Nov 2026

The short answer

Whether you can combine two OET sittings depends only on the organisation that will read your results. Here is the position in one glance.

Publish a two-sitting rule

  • UK: NMC (nurses, midwives, nursing associates).
  • Australia: Ahpra and the National Boards; ANMAC (nursing skills assessment).
  • Ireland: CORU (physiotherapy, occupational therapy, dietetics, optometry, podiatry, radiography, speech and language therapy).
  • New Zealand: Medical Council, Nursing Council, Pharmacy Council, Physiotherapy Board, Occupational Therapy Board.
  • Canada: the nursing regulator in Manitoba (CRNM) refers to two sittings, but its OET scores are not confirmed.

Require one sitting

  • UK: GMC (doctors), GPhC (pharmacists).
  • USA: ECFMG (doctors).
  • Ireland: Medical Council, NMBI (nurses and midwives), PSI (pharmacists).
  • New Zealand: Dental Council, Optometrists and Dispensing Opticians Board, Veterinary Council.
  • Canada: CPSBC (doctors, British Columbia), CNO (nurses, Ontario), the NAPRA model policy for pharmacists.
  • Singapore: the medical, pharmacy, dental and allied health councils.

Something else, or silent

  • HCPC (UK): accepts a retake of one element within six months. Combining two full sittings is not stated.
  • Not confirmed: the Australian Department of Home Affairs (visas), TruMerit (US nurses), NABP (US pharmacists) and several smaller boards say nothing about combining OET sittings.
Before you rely on any of this

A regulator can change its English language rule at any time. Treat this page as a map, then read the regulator's own page (linked in every section below) on the day you book and again on the day you apply.

What clubbing means, and where OET stands

"Clubbing" is the word candidates use, and OET uses it too. In its Test Regulations, OET says that some recognising organisations accept clubbing of scores from tests taken on different dates, and tells candidates to check with their recognising organisation whether results must come from one sitting.

What OET itself says

  • The decision belongs to the regulator, not to OET.
  • Your first attempt must include all four sub-tests (Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking). Without that you do not receive results.
  • Each sitting produces a Statement of Results with a score from 0 to 500 for each sub-test.
  • You share results with an organisation through your online OET account.
  • How long a result stays valid is decided by the organisation that recognises it, not by OET.

What OET does not say

  • One combined certificate: not confirmed. Neither the Test Regulations nor OET's results page says that OET merges two sittings into one document.
  • Our reading, which is an inference and not an OET statement: each sitting keeps its own Statement of Results, you share each one, and the regulator does the combining.
  • We found no OET help article that explains clubbing step by step. Ask your regulator how it wants to receive two sittings.
Grades and scores

The NMC prints the bands like this: grade B is 350 to 440, grade C+ is 300 to 340 and grade C is 250 to 290. Some regulators write letters, some write numbers, and a few now use numbers that sit inside a band, such as 360 or 320.

The regulators that allow combining, one by one

Scores are written in the order Listening, Reading, Writing, Speaking unless a line says otherwise.

NMC (UK): nurses, midwives, nursing associates

  • Target: grade B (350 or above) in Listening, Reading and Speaking, and grade C+ (300 or above) in Writing. Across two sittings you need B in each of those three at least once, and one Writing score of at least C+.
  • Floor in every sitting: at least C+ (300) in Listening, Reading and Speaking, and at least C (250) in Writing.
  • Sittings and window: two sittings, taken within 12 months of each other.
  • All four sub-tests each time: yes. You cannot take or retake a single domain.
  • Formats: paper, computer and OET@Home, and OET@Home can be combined with a paper or computer sitting. OET Nursing only. OET cannot be combined with IELTS.
  • Validity clock: scores are valid for two years, and both sittings must fall within the two years before the NMC assesses your complete application.
  • Source: NMC, accepted English language tests: OET (last updated 8 March 2024).

Ahpra and the National Boards (Australia)

  • Target, tests taken on or after 23 April 2026: Listening 350, Reading 360, Writing 350, Speaking 360.
  • Floor in every sitting, same tests: Listening 320, Reading 340, Writing 350, Speaking 350.
  • Tests taken on or before 22 April 2026: B in Listening, Reading and Speaking and C+ in Writing, with no component below C+ in either sitting. Which table applies depends on the date of the test, not the date you apply.
  • Sittings and window: one sitting, or a maximum of two within a 12-month period.
  • All four sub-tests each time: yes. Ahpra accepts a one-skill retake for IELTS only, not for OET.
  • Formats: paper, or computer at a test centre. OET@Home is not accepted. Results from different test providers cannot be combined.
  • Validity clock: two years before the date you lodge your application. With two sittings, the two years run from the earlier one.
  • Professions: the same scores for every National Board except the Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Health Practice Board.
  • Source: Ahpra, accepted English language tests (page reviewed 23 April 2026).

ANMAC (Australia): nursing and midwifery skills assessment

  • Target: B in Listening, Reading and Speaking and C+ in Writing. The page still prints letter grades.
  • Floor in every sitting: no component below C+.
  • Sittings and window: one sitting, or a maximum of two in a 12-month period.
  • All four sub-tests each time: yes.
  • Formats: paper or computer tests taken in an authorised testing centre, from the same test provider.
  • Validity clock: results must be less than two years old.
  • Not confirmed: whether ANMAC now applies Ahpra's numbers of 23 April 2026. Its page has not published numeric equivalents and announces coming changes, so re-read it before you apply.
  • Source: ANMAC, full skills assessment (no page date).

CORU (Ireland): seven health and social care professions

  • Target: B (350) in three components and C+ (300) in one for physiotherapy, occupational therapy, dietetics, optometry and podiatry. Radiography needs B (350) in all four. Speech and language therapy needs B at 400 in three components and B at 350 in one.
  • Floor in every sitting: no OET sub-test below C+. The page adds that the overall minimum score must be met on both sittings. Ask CORU how it applies that sentence to your scores.
  • Sittings and window: a maximum of two sittings. The repeat must be with the same test provider and within six months of the first test.
  • All four sub-tests each time: yes. CORU does not accept individual OET sub-tests.
  • Formats: the page's sentence on at-home tests is ambiguous, so OET@Home is not confirmed.
  • Validity clock: certificates dated within two years of the date you submit your application.
  • Source: CORU, language requirements (no page date).

Medical Council of New Zealand: doctors

  • Target: Speaking 350, Listening 350, Reading 350, Writing 300, in the OET Medicine test.
  • Floor in every sitting: no component below 300.
  • Sittings and window: one sitting, or a maximum of two in a 12-month period.
  • All four sub-tests each time: yes.
  • Formats: the policy does not mention OET@Home or the computer test: not confirmed.
  • Validity clock: within two years of your application for registration, or of your NZREX Clinical date.
  • Source: MCNZ, policy on English language requirements (September 2026).

Nursing Council of New Zealand: nurses

  • Target: 350 in Reading, Listening and Speaking and 300 in Writing.
  • Floor in every sitting: none is printed.
  • Sittings and window: the scores can be achieved over more than one sitting. All required scores must be met within 12 months of the first sitting, and all sittings must be completed within three years of starting your application.
  • Formats: an OET test centre, on paper or on computer. OET@Home is not accepted. OET and IELTS results cannot be mixed.
  • Source: Nursing Council, English language competence policy guidance (19 August 2022).

Occupational Therapy Board of New Zealand

  • Target: B in each of the four components, in OET Occupational Therapy.
  • Sittings and window: you may combine (club) more than one attempt, but the standard must be reached within 12 months of sitting the first test.
  • Floor in every sitting: none is printed.
  • Validity clock: results no older than 24 months when submitted. OET@Home: not confirmed.
  • Source: OTBNZ, English language policy for registration (policy 704, version 2.0).

New Zealand: Pharmacy Council and Physiotherapy Board

  • Pharmacy Council: A or B in each of the four bands within two years of the application. If you do not reach every score at one sitting, results from earlier OET tests within a two-year period are accepted. The page prints no floor and no number of sittings. Source.
  • Physiotherapy Board: 350 in Reading, Listening and Speaking and 300 in Writing, from results less than two years old. OET scores may be obtained across two sittings in six months. Source (help portal, updated 5 June 2025).
  • Not confirmed for the Physiotherapy Board: the detail that all four areas must be sat both times with nothing below 300. We could read it only in a search extract, because the Board's own page blocked us. The help portal also says the Board does not accept composite results from multiple examinations, so ask the Board to confirm before you plan on two sittings.
Canada: one reference to two sittings

The College of Registered Nurses of Manitoba's accepted measures document (effective 26 March 2025) asks for a language test within the past two years and mentions achieving the minimum scores across two sittings of the same test. The document does not print the test list or the OET scores, so both are not confirmed. The other Canadian regulators whose sitting rule we could read ask for one sitting.

Single sub-test retakes are not the same as clubbing

Clubbing combines two full sittings. A single sub-test retake means sitting only one part again, for example Writing alone. OET offers it. Most regulators do not accept it.

What OET offers

  • After a first test with all four sub-tests, OET's Test Regulations allow a candidate to book fewer than four on a later attempt, subject to the recognising organisation's requirements.
  • In a news post of 29 October 2026, OET confirmed the option to retake an individual sub-test and told nurses to check whether their credentialling organisation and state board accept it.
  • From January 2027, OET on Paper continues for Nursing and Medicine only. Any retake option that depends on the paper test is therefore open only to those two professions. Everyone else books on computer.

What regulators accept

  • HCPC (UK): the only regulator we found that says it accepts an OET retake of one element. The retake must be completed within six months of the original test date. Source (page updated 5 November 2026).
  • Not accepted: the NMC, Ahpra, ANMAC, the Medical Council of New Zealand and CORU all require the four sub-tests in every sitting. The PSI in Ireland and the Dental Council of New Zealand say they do not accept one-skill retakes.
  • Not confirmed: TruMerit and the US state boards of nursing. TruMerit's pages are silent, and we did not research the state boards one by one.
HCPC scores, for reference

The HCPC lists OET for podiatry, dietetics, occupational therapy, physiotherapy and radiography at a total of 1400 with no element below 300, and for speech and language therapy at a total of 1800 with no element below 400. It names the computer and paper tests. OET@Home and the combining of two full sittings are not stated on its page: not confirmed.

Common mistakes

Mistakes about the rule

  • Assuming every regulator clubs. The GMC, GPhC, ECFMG and many others do not.
  • Carrying one regulator's floor to another. The NMC's floors are C+, C+, C in Writing and C+. Ahpra's are 320, 340, 350 and 350. ANMAC's is nothing below C+.
  • Reading the target and forgetting the floor. One weak sub-test in the first sitting can make that sitting unusable.
  • Mixing OET with IELTS. The regulators that club want both results from the same test.

Mistakes about timing and format

  • Missing the window. 12 months for the NMC, Ahpra, ANMAC and the Medical Council of New Zealand. Six months for CORU and the Physiotherapy Board of New Zealand.
  • Forgetting the validity clock. For Ahpra the two years run from the earlier sitting, so a slow second sitting shortens the time you have to apply.
  • Booking one sub-test only. A single sub-test retake does not count for the NMC or Ahpra.
  • Using OET@Home where it is refused. The NMC accepts it. Ahpra, ANMAC and the Nursing Council of New Zealand do not.
  • Meeting registration but missing the visa. The Australian Department of Home Affairs asks for Writing 380 for Proficient English, while Ahpra registration asks for Writing 350.

What is not confirmed

We could not confirm these points from the organisations' own pages on 27 November 2026. Do not plan around them until the organisation tells you in writing.

OET and the UK

  • Whether OET issues a single combined certificate for two sittings.
  • HCPC: combining two full sittings, and OET@Home.
  • GDC: its guidance names only IELTS and does not mention OET at all.

Australia, USA and elsewhere

  • Department of Home Affairs (Australian visas): combining OET sittings. Its pages name retake schemes for other tests only.
  • ANMAC: numeric scores and whether the 23 April 2026 Ahpra figures apply.
  • TruMerit and US state boards of nursing: combining and single sub-test retakes.
  • NABP (US pharmacists): OET is accepted for tests taken on or after 11 January 2027, but the minimum scores, sitting rules and validity had not been published when we checked.
  • PSI super-scoring, the Dental Council of Ireland, the Veterinary Council of Ireland, and in New Zealand the radiography, dietetics and podiatry boards: no combining rule for OET could be read.
  • Gulf regulators: we found no published OET requirement on their websites, so there is no clubbing rule to report.

How to plan a second sitting

If your first result was close, a second sitting can finish the job, but only when it is planned around your regulator's rule.

Five steps

  • 1. Name your regulator. Find it in the recognition matrix by profession and country and read its current scores in OET score requirements.
  • 2. Test your first sitting against the floor. If one sub-test is under the floor, that sitting cannot be combined. Put your numbers into the OET score calculator.
  • 3. Count the window and the validity clock. Choose a date from OET test dates and fees that sits inside both.
  • 4. Choose a format your regulator accepts. Read the OET@Home guide before booking a home test, and remember that paper is for Nursing and Medicine only from January 2027.
  • 5. Book all four sub-tests unless your regulator says in writing that it accepts a single sub-test retake.

Frequently asked questions

What is OET clubbing?

Clubbing means using the results of two OET sittings together to meet a regulator's score requirement. OET does not decide whether it is allowed: each regulator sets its own rule, and many accept one sitting only.

Which regulators allow OET clubbing?

On the pages we read on 27 November 2026, the NMC, Ahpra and ANMAC publish two-sitting rules. So do CORU in Ireland and, in New Zealand, the Medical Council, the Nursing Council, the Pharmacy Council, the Physiotherapy Board and the Occupational Therapy Board. Each one sets different conditions.

Can I combine two OET results for the GMC?

No. The GMC asks for at least grade B in each of the four areas in the same test, from your most recent sitting.

How far apart can the two sittings be?

It depends on the regulator. The NMC, Ahpra, ANMAC and the Medical Council of New Zealand use a 12-month window. CORU and the Physiotherapy Board of New Zealand use six months.

Can I retake only one OET sub-test?

OET lets you book fewer than four sub-tests after a first full test, but most regulators do not accept the result. The NMC, Ahpra, ANMAC, the Medical Council of New Zealand and CORU want all four sub-tests in every sitting. The HCPC is the only regulator we found that accepts a retake of one element, within six months of the original test.

Does OET issue one certificate for two sittings?

This is not confirmed. OET's published pages do not say that it merges two sittings into one Statement of Results. Our reading is that each sitting keeps its own Statement of Results and the regulator does the combining. Confirm this with your regulator.

Can I combine an OET sitting with an IELTS sitting?

We found no regulator that allows it. The NMC, Ahpra, ANMAC, CORU and the Nursing Council of New Zealand all say both results must come from the same test.

Does OET@Home count in a combined result?

For the NMC, yes: it accepts OET@Home and lets you combine it with a paper or computer sitting. Ahpra, ANMAC and the Nursing Council of New Zealand do not accept OET@Home at all.
