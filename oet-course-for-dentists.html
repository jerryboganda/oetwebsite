Who is this OET course for dentists for?

It is for dentists who need OET for registration abroad and want one structured route through all four sub-tests, with personal feedback on the two that are written for dentists.

Dentists registering in Australia

The Dental Board of Australia applies the same accepted English tests and minimum scores as the other National Boards, and OET is one of them. The Australia pathway guide explains where the English test fits in the registration steps.

Dentists heading to Ireland or New Zealand

The Dental Council of Ireland asks for at least B in each OET Dentistry sub-test, and the Dental Council of New Zealand asks for A or B in each, in one sitting. One weak sub-test means another full test, so targeted feedback matters.

Dentists planning the UK

Read the GDC's English language page before you book anything: it names IELTS, not OET. Our OET vs IELTS and OET vs PTE guides compare the tests if you are still deciding.

What is in OET Dentistry?

OET has four sub-tests. OET says Listening and Reading are common to all candidates, while Writing and Speaking are profession specific and reflect workplace scenarios from your own profession.

Sub-testTimeWhat dentists doDental content?
ListeningAbout 40 minutes, 42 questionsThree parts on topics of general healthcare interest; Part A is note completion from consultations, using the words you hearNo, the same for every profession
Reading60 minutes, 42 questionsThree parts; Part A is a 15-minute task across four short texts, collected before you start Parts B and CNo, the same for every profession
Writing45 minutes: 5 minutes reading time, then 40 minutes writingOne letter from dental case notes, such as a referral, advice, transfer or discharge letter, with a guide of 180–200 wordsYes
SpeakingAbout 20 minutesTwo role-plays with an interlocutor playing a patient or a patient's relative or carer; three minutes to prepare each cardYes

OET Dentistry can be taken on paper or on computer at a venue. OET@Home also exists, but Ahpra and the Dental Council of Ireland do not accept it, so check your regulator before you book. OET publishes a free Dentistry sample pack, including a Speaking role card; find it through our guide to official OET sample tests.

What OET score do dentists need?

There is no single OET pass mark. Each dental regulator sets its own English rule, and they differ more than most candidates expect.

How do dentists pass OET Writing?

You have 5 minutes to read the case notes and 40 minutes to write one letter, with a body of about 180–200 words. OET applies no automatic penalty for writing a little over or under, but a much longer letter usually means notes the reader did not need.

Referral to a specialist

Refer a patient to an oral and maxillofacial surgeon, a periodontist, an orthodontist or a paediatric dentist. Say what you found, what you have already done, and exactly what you want the specialist to do.

Letters to a doctor

Write to a GP about a finding, or ask for information about a patient's medical history or medicines before treatment. Keep dental terms to what a medical reader uses, and make the question easy to answer.

Letters to non-dental readers

OET's own Dentistry sample letter is written to a retirement village, asking for closer supervision of a resident's oral hygiene. Carers and parents need plain, practical instructions, not clinical shorthand.

What the assessors check

Six criteria: Purpose, Content, Conciseness & Clarity, Genre & Style, Organisation & Layout, and Language. A clear purpose in the opening sentence and a specific request at the end protect marks across several of them.

Practice example: an urgent referral from a dental practice

This is an invented practice case written by OET with Dr Hesham to demonstrate the method. It is not an official OET task and does not describe a real patient.

Practice example: the task

You are the dentist at a general dental practice. Write a letter of referral to the Oral and Maxillofacial Surgery department at the local hospital, requesting urgent assessment of a lesion on Mr K's tongue.

Practice example: case notes (summary)
  • Mr K, 58, bus driver; married, two adult children
  • Smoker, 20 cigarettes a day for 35 years; alcohol about 25 units a week
  • Type 2 diabetes, metformin 500 mg twice daily; no known drug allergies
  • 3 weeks ago: painless ulcer, left lateral border of tongue, about 8 mm; sharp cusp on lower left first molar smoothed
  • Today: ulcer not healed, now about 10 mm with firm edges; no other mucosal lesions seen
  • Scale and polish 6 months ago; bridge for upper incisors being planned
  • Prefers morning appointments; says he is nervous about hospitals
Keep, because the surgeon needs it
  • Site, size and duration of the ulcer, and that it has grown
  • Firm edges, and no healing after the sharp cusp was smoothed
  • Smoking and alcohol history
  • Diabetes and metformin, relevant to any procedure
  • His nervousness about hospitals, in one short sentence
Omit, because it does not change the request
  • Scale and polish, and the planned bridge
  • His job and family
  • His preference for morning appointments
Paragraph plan
  • P1, purpose: urgent assessment of a non-healing tongue ulcer
  • P2, the lesion: site, size, firm edges, growth, and no healing after the cusp was smoothed
  • P3, relevant history: smoking, alcohol, diabetes and metformin
  • P4, closing request: urgent assessment and investigation; mention his nervousness
Model opening sentence

I am writing to refer Mr K, a 58-year-old man, for urgent assessment of a non-healing ulcer on the left lateral border of his tongue, which has persisted for three weeks despite removal of a possible source of trauma.

Key takeaway

The first sentence tells the surgeon why the referral is urgent. Every kept note supports that urgency or helps the team plan safely; everything else, however true, stays in the notes.

The same keep-or-omit method is explained step by step in OET referral letter structure and the OET Writing tips. To see exactly which notes and sentences are costing you marks, send your letters for OET writing correction.

What dental role-plays should you practise for OET Speaking?

OET Speaking takes about 20 minutes and has two role-plays. You get three minutes to prepare each card, and a role-play usually ends at around five minutes. The interlocutor plays a patient or a patient's relative or carer; OET's free Dentistry sample role-play is set in a local dental clinic.

Dental scenarios worth practising

  • Explaining a diagnosis and the treatment options, such as a filling, root canal treatment or an extraction
  • Aftercare advice following an extraction, and when to seek help
  • Reassuring an anxious patient who has avoided the dentist for years
  • Oral hygiene and diet advice for a parent whose child has tooth decay
  • Talking about smoking and gum disease, or with a patient who expects antibiotics for toothache
  • Responding calmly to a patient who is unhappy about a delay, a cost or a result

How you are assessed

Two assessors score the recording on linguistic criteria (intelligibility, fluency, appropriateness of language, and resources of grammar and expression) and on clinical communication (relationship building, understanding and incorporating the patient's perspective, providing structure, information gathering and information giving). Plain explanations matter more than technical vocabulary: say "gum disease" before "periodontitis", then check the patient has understood.

Build the structure with the OET Speaking tips and empathy and signposting, practise the anxious-patient card with handling an angry or anxious patient, then rehearse dental cards aloud before your private session.

How should dentists prepare for Listening and Reading?

You sit exactly the same Listening and Reading papers as doctors and nurses, so dental knowledge does not help much here. Method does.

Listening (about 40 minutes, 42 questions)

In Part A you complete notes from consultations using the words you hear, so spelling and prediction matter. Practise a little every day rather than one long session a week. See the OET Listening tips.

Reading (60 minutes, 42 questions)

Part A is collected after 15 minutes, so train fast scanning across four short texts; Parts B and C reward careful reading and eliminating distractors. See the OET Reading tips.

OET for dentists FAQ

Short answers before you enrol. More exam questions are answered on the full OET FAQ page.

Can dentists use OET for UK registration?

The GDC's English language page does not name OET. It names IELTS, including IELTS One Skill Retake and IELTS Online, and its guidance asks for Academic IELTS with 7 overall and at least 6.5 in each area. Check the GDC page before you book any test for UK registration.

What OET score do dentists need for Australia?

The Dental Board of Australia uses Ahpra's accepted test rules. For tests taken on or after 23 April 2026 the OET minimums are Listening 350, Reading 360, Writing 350 and Speaking 360, on paper or computer at a test centre. OET@Home is not accepted.

What OET grade does the Dental Council of Ireland require?

At least grade B in each of Listening, Reading, Writing and Speaking in OET Dentistry, with a certificate no more than 2 years old when you apply. The Council accepts paper-based and computer-based OET but not OET@Home.

Does the Dental Council of New Zealand accept OET?

Yes. The Dental Council of New Zealand has approved OET and IELTS Academic. Dentists need A or B grades in each of the four OET sub-tests, passed in one sitting, and a pass is valid for two years.

Which OET course should a dentist choose?

Dentistry is covered by the Modified Allied Health course family, which includes every profession outside Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy and Physiotherapy. Book OET Dentistry for the exam itself, and practise dental letters and role-plays alongside the course.

What do dentists write in OET Writing?

One letter from dental case notes in 40 minutes, after 5 minutes of reading time, with a body of about 180 to 200 words. It may be a referral to a specialist, a letter to a doctor or advice to carers. OET's own Dentistry sample letter is written to a retirement village about a resident's oral hygiene.
