OET@Home in 2027: who can book it, and which regulators accept it

OET@Home is the full OET test taken on your own computer with remote proctoring. Two separate rules decide whether it is any use to you. OET offers it only where no test venue is available, or as a case-by-case exception. And your regulator decides whether it accepts the result: several do not.

This guide keeps three questions apart: can you book it, will your regulator accept it, and is “online Speaking” the same thing (it is not). Last verified: 27 November 2026.

What OET@Home is

OET is delivered in three modes: the OET Test on Paper, the OET Test on Computer at a venue, and OET@Home (OET Test Regulations). The content, timing and marking are the same. What differs is where you sit and how you are supervised.

How it runs

  • All four sub-tests are taken at home on your own computer, with full remote proctoring (OET@Home page).
  • Prometric delivers the test. You install its ProProctor application for Listening, Reading and Writing.
  • Speaking is a live role-play with a human interlocutor on the OET Speaking platform, on a different day in the same test administration.

Fee and booking

  • The fee OET shows is AU$587, the same as the other two modes. In the United States it is US$455. OET publishes no local-currency price and says nothing about tax.
  • Booking closes 7 days before test day.
  • Changing or cancelling a booking costs AU$120 or AU$200 depending on timing: see the cancellation and refund rules.

Question 1: can I book OET@Home from my country?

Usually only if your country has no OET test venue. This is OET's rule, and it has nothing to do with your regulator.

The no-venue rule

OET's page says OET@Home is “offered in countries where taking the test at a venue is not yet available”. The Test Regulations (section 6.2) list the same condition first: sitting the test where there is no venue available.

The case-by-case exception

The second condition in the regulations is an exception approved case by case by a senior OET staff member. The example OET gives is a disability or medical condition. You apply through OET Support, and approval is not automatic.

Book where you physically sit

You must book in the same country in which you will physically sit the test, and OET asks for proof of residency to show that you are physically in that country. Moving a booking to another country means cancelling and booking again.

There is no official country list

OET publishes no list of countries where OET@Home can or cannot be booked. The regulations add that not all test dates and testing modes are available in all countries, and that OET does not test candidates residing in sanctioned countries.

How to check for your country

Search your city in the official OET booking tool and look at the test modes it offers you. Our OET test centres directory shows which countries have venues. If your country has a venue, for example Egypt, Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Qatar, Kuwait or Oman, plan for a test at a venue: under OET's rule you cannot normally book OET@Home there.

Which professions can take OET@Home?

The two official sources disagree, and neither page is dated.

oet.com: all 12

OET's own OET@Home page lists all 12 professions: Medicine, Nursing, Dentistry, Dietetics, Pharmacy, Physiotherapy, Podiatry, Radiology, Occupational Therapy, Veterinary Science, Optometry and Speech Pathology.

Cambridge English: four

The Cambridge English overview of OET lists four professions for OET@Home: Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy and Physiotherapy.

What to do about the conflict

Which page is current is not confirmed. Check your own profession in the booking tool before you rely on OET@Home, and do not plan around it until the tool actually offers you a date.

Question 2: will my regulator accept OET@Home?

That is your regulator's decision, not OET's. OET itself warns that OET@Home may not be accepted by all recognising organisations, and it publishes no list of who accepts which mode. The table uses four statuses:

The four statuses

  • Accepted: the regulator's own page says it accepts OET@Home.
  • Conditional: accepted only in the situation the regulator describes.
  • Not accepted: the regulator's own page says it does not accept OET@Home or at-home tests.
  • Not confirmed: the regulator's page does not state a position on OET@Home, or we could not read it. Treat this as “ask the regulator in writing”, never as a yes.

Regulator pages last checked: .

United Kingdom

The GMC (doctors, OET Medicine) and the NMC (nurses and midwives) accept OET@Home. The GPhC (pharmacists) does not. The HCPC names only OET on Computer and OET on Paper, so OET@Home is not confirmed: treat it as not accepted unless the HCPC tells you otherwise in writing. The GDC page does not name OET at all, so nothing is confirmed for dentists.

Ireland

NMBI (nurses and midwives), the Medical Council (doctors) and the PSI (pharmacists) do not accept it. CORU (health and social care professionals) says online or at-home versions are accepted for OET, although its page does not use the name OET@Home. Because the page never names OET@Home, the table lists CORU as not confirmed: ask CORU in writing before you rely on it.

Australia

Ahpra and the National Boards do not accept OET@Home: they accept OET on paper, or on computer in a testing centre. The Department of Home Affairs does not accept it for visas either.

New Zealand

The Nursing Council of New Zealand does not accept online tests such as OET@Home. For the Medical Council, the Pharmacy Council and the Physiotherapy Board it is not confirmed: their pages say nothing about the delivery mode.

United States

ECFMG (doctors) is conditional: OET@Home only for applicants with no physical test venue in their country. TruMerit, formerly CGFNS (nurses), does not accept at-home versions of English tests. NABP (pharmacists, FPGEC) does not accept home editions of OET or TOEFL iBT; it accepts OET Pharmacy only for tests taken on or after 11 January 2027, at an approved computer-based test centre in the United States, its territories or Canada. See OET for pharmacists in the USA. FCCPT (physical therapists) and the state nursing boards: not confirmed.

Canada

BCCNM (nurses in British Columbia) accepts OET in person only. The national pharmacy policy of NAPRA is conditional: remotely proctored tests can be accepted if they keep the same level of security, each provincial pharmacy regulator decides, and the policy does not name OET@Home. The College of Nurses of Ontario, CPSO, MCC, NNAS and PEBC: not confirmed.

Singapore

Not confirmed. The Singapore Medical Council and the Singapore Pharmacy Council state OET scores but no delivery mode, and we found no Singapore Nursing Board page listing accepted English tests.

Gulf countries

Not confirmed. The Gulf regulators we checked publish no rule on OET@Home. Where an employer asks for OET, ask that employer which mode it accepts. These countries also have OET venues, so OET@Home is normally not bookable there in the first place.

Before you pay

Ask your regulator, in writing, whether it accepts OET@Home for your profession and route. OET gives the same instruction in its regulations: check with your recognising organisation that the test mode you have chosen is accepted. Score rules are in OET score requirements, and the rules on combining sittings are in OET clubbing rules.

Question 3: is online Speaking the same as OET@Home?

No. A test is OET@Home only when all four sub-tests are taken at home under remote proctoring. A venue-based test can still include Speaking by video.

OET on Computer at a venue

Listening, Reading and Writing are sat in an approved test centre. Speaking is, in OET's words, “conducted at home via a secure platform or for select countries, conducted at venue” (OET on Computer). OET lists the countries with Speaking at a venue. This is still OET on Computer, not OET@Home.

Regulators treat the two differently

Ahpra does not accept OET@Home, yet it states that the computer test taken in a testing centre, with its one remotely proctored element, is acceptable. The Nursing Council of New Zealand refuses online tests but accepts OET on computer at an approved venue with the Speaking sub-test hosted online.

Paper tests from January 2027

From January 2027 paper candidates (Nursing and Medicine only) still attend a venue for Speaking, and depending on the venue they meet the interlocutor in person or over a live video connection. That is also not OET@Home. Details: the January 2027 paper-test changes.

One regulator to ask

The PSI in Ireland says home-based tests are not accepted and notes that Speaking is delivered at home for OET on Computer candidates, without saying in so many words that this is acceptable. If you are a pharmacist applying to the PSI, confirm with the PSI before you book.

When you book OET on Computer

OET's booking page asks you to make sure the Speaking mode you select is accepted by the organisation you are applying to. The two venue formats are compared in OET on computer vs paper.

Equipment, room and ID rules

These are the rules OET states in its Test Regulations (section 6.2) and its OET@Home test day guide of 19 June 2026. Exact system figures are in the OET@Home User Guide, which we have not quoted here.

Equipment

  • A computer with a video-enabled camera, speakers and a microphone for all sub-tests.
  • Prometric's ProProctor application for Listening, Reading and Writing, and a secure browser for Speaking.
  • A wired headset for Listening. Bluetooth headphones are not allowed.
  • No headphones during the Speaking sub-test.
  • One screen only: additional monitors are not allowed. VPNs, virtual machines and Bluetooth must be off.

Room

  • A walled, indoor, well-lit space with no transparent glass walls or partitions.
  • You must be alone in a quiet room, with no CCTV.
  • All tests are monitored by video and audio recording.
  • For Speaking, the interlocutor checks you in and carries out a 360-degree check of the room.

ID

  • You upload an image of your ID when you book, and you must show the same, original document on test day. Copies are not accepted.
  • A valid passport, or a valid national ID issued by the government of the country where you sit the test, in English lettering.
  • A driving licence is not an acceptable ID, with one exception OET states for candidates testing in the United States.

On the day

  • Log on at least 15 minutes before your scheduled start time.
  • There is one scheduled 10-minute break, after Reading Part A only.
  • Speaking is on a different day in the same test administration.

Sources: OET Test Regulations, OET@Home test day guide, test day guide, part 3 (Speaking), test day information.

When do OET@Home results arrive?

OET says OET@Home results can arrive as early as 48 hours after you have completed all sub-tests. That is the earliest case, not a promise.

Plan with a margin

For the computer test at a venue OET adds that 95% of results are released within 6 calendar days. Whether that figure also applies to OET@Home is not confirmed, because the OET@Home page does not state it. ECFMG, for its own applicants, says OET Medicine results typically reach it 10 business days after the test. If you have a registration deadline, count back from the slowest of these.

OET@Home FAQ

What is OET@Home?

OET@Home is the full OET test taken at home on your own computer with remote proctoring. It is one of three delivery modes, next to the OET Test on Paper and the OET Test on Computer at a venue, and it has the same content, timing and marking.

Can I take OET@Home from any country?

No. OET offers OET@Home where taking the test at a venue is not yet available, or as an exception approved case by case. OET publishes no country list, so check the official booking tool for your city. If your country has a test venue, plan to sit at a venue.

Which professions can take OET@Home?

The official sources disagree. OET's own page lists all 12 professions, while the Cambridge English overview lists four: Medicine, Nursing, Pharmacy and Physiotherapy. Check your profession in the booking tool before you rely on OET@Home.

Which regulators accept OET@Home?

On their own pages, the GMC and the NMC accept it, and ECFMG accepts it only when your country has no physical test venue. CORU says at-home versions are accepted for OET without naming OET@Home, so we list it as not confirmed. The GPhC, NMBI, the Medical Council of Ireland, the PSI, Ahpra, the Australian Department of Home Affairs, the Nursing Council of New Zealand, TruMerit, NABP and BCCNM do not. For many others it is not confirmed, so ask your regulator in writing.

Is online Speaking the same as OET@Home?

No. On the OET Test on Computer you sit Listening, Reading and Writing at a venue, and Speaking may be taken at home or at a venue depending on the country. That is still a venue-based test. OET@Home means all four sub-tests are taken at home.

How much does OET@Home cost?

OET shows a fee of AU$587 for OET@Home, the same as the paper and computer tests. In the United States the fee is US$455. OET does not publish local-currency prices or say whether tax is added.

How fast are OET@Home results?

OET says results can arrive as early as 48 hours after you complete all sub-tests. That is the earliest case, not a guarantee, so leave a margin before any registration deadline.

What equipment do I need for OET@Home?

A computer with a camera, speakers and a microphone, Prometric's ProProctor application, a wired headset for Listening and a single screen. You must be alone in a quiet, walled, well-lit room and show the original ID you used to book.
