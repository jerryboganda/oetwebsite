What you get for every corrected OET letter

OET writing correction only helps when the feedback is specific enough to act on. Each manual letter assessment gives you four things.

An estimated score

Every letter is given an estimated score, so you can see whether your writing is near the grade your regulator asks for, or still some way off, before you book or rebook the exam.

Detailed correction

Your letter is corrected in detail: which case notes did not belong, where the structure lost the reader, which sentences need rewording, and which language errors repeat.

Voice-note feedback on WhatsApp

Dr Hesham explains the corrections in a voice note sent via WhatsApp. Hearing why a choice cost marks is often what makes the fix stick on the next letter.

Letters you choose, or recall-based tasks

Submit letters you have chosen yourself, or work through recall-recommended letters so your practice reflects the letter types candidates are meeting in recent exams.

How OET writing correction works

The process is simple. What matters is that you write under exam conditions and use every piece of feedback on the next letter.

01

Write under exam conditions

Use the five minutes of reading time, then write the letter in the 40 minutes allowed without notes or templates. A letter written slowly with help tells you very little about your exam score.

02

Submit it for assessment

Send your letter with the case notes it answers. Manual assessments in the letter add-ons are returned within 48–72 hours, excluding Fridays.

03

Read the corrections, then listen to the voice note

Go through the marked letter first, then listen to the explanation. Write down the two or three errors that cost you most; they are your focus for the next letter.

04

Rewrite, then write a new letter

Rewrite the same letter with the corrections applied, then attempt a different case. Improvement shows when the fixes hold on a task you have not seen before.

What your letter is marked against

OET Writing is assessed against six published criteria. Corrections and feedback are organised around the same areas, so you know which part of the letter is holding your score back.

Purpose

Can the reader tell straight away why you are writing and what you need them to do? The purpose should be clear in the opening and supported to the end.

Content

Have you included the case notes this reader needs, accurately, and left out what they do not? Relevance matters more than completeness.

Conciseness and clarity

Is the information summarised efficiently, without padding or repetition, so the reader can take it in quickly?

Genre and style

Is the register right for a professional letter and for this particular reader, whether a specialist, a nurse, a care home or a patient?

Organisation and layout

Are paragraphs grouped logically and ordered by importance, with a layout the reader can follow without searching?

Language

Are grammar, vocabulary, spelling and punctuation accurate enough that nothing gets in the way of the meaning?

Common reasons OET letters miss a B

Most candidates who are stuck at C or C+ are not short of English. They repeat the same few habits, and correction is the quickest way to spot them.

The purpose is buried

The reader reaches the second paragraph before learning why the letter was written or what is being asked of them.

Case notes are copied, not selected

Every note is transferred into the letter, including history the reader does not need, which hides the information that matters.

The tone does not fit the reader

A letter to a patient's relative written like a specialist referral, or a referral that sounds casual, loses marks in genre and style.

Information is out of order

Paragraphs follow the order of the case notes instead of the order the reader needs, so the urgent point arrives late.

The closing request is vague

The letter ends without a clear request or next step, which weakens purpose even when the rest is strong.

Small language errors repeat

Tense, articles and sentence boundaries go wrong in the same way on every letter. Once someone points the pattern out, it is usually quick to fix.

OET writing correction FAQ

Quick answers before you send your first letter.

How long does OET writing correction take?

Writing letter assessment add-ons are marked manually and returned within 48–72 hours, excluding Fridays. Writing Assessment Packages in the learner app typically release a detailed assessment after a minimum 15-minute review window.

Who marks my OET letters?

Manual letter assessments are marked by Dr Ahmed Hesham. Letters submitted through the app's Writing Assessment Packages are assessed using Dr Hesham's lectures and Rulebook with a second quality review, and a human mentor review is available when required or requested.

What feedback do I get on each letter?

Each manually assessed letter comes back with an estimated score, detailed corrections and a voice-note explanation sent via WhatsApp. You can submit letters you choose yourself or letters recommended from recent recalls.

Do I need to be enrolled in a course to get my letters corrected?

The 3, 5, 7 and 10-letter assessment add-ons are for candidates enrolled in an eligible course and follow that course's access window. The Recorded Writing Crash Course bundles and the app's Writing Assessment Packages can be bought on their own.

Is OET writing correction available for nurses, doctors and pharmacists?

Yes. Manual letter assessments cover doctors, nurses and pharmacy candidates, the Writing Crash Course is available for Medicine, Nursing and Pharmacy, and the app's Writing Assessment Packages are open to all disciplines.
