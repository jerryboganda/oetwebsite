What is a nursing referral letter in OET Writing?

OET Writing gives you 5 minutes to read the case notes and 40 minutes to write one letter, with a body of about 180–200 words. In a nursing referral, you ask another health professional, often a specialist nurse or clinic, to take on part of the patient's care, so the request and the reason for it must be clear from the first paragraph. This page gives you a full practice task, a model answer and an explanation of every selection decision.

Reading time: 5 minutes

You read the case notes and the task before the writing time starts. Use it to decide who the reader is and what they must do next.

Writing time: 40 minutes

You write one letter. A useful split is 5 minutes planning, 30 minutes writing and 5 minutes checking purpose, dates and doses.

How to use this sample

Read the case notes, write your own letter under time and only then compare it with the model answer. Copying the model teaches far less than finding your own selection mistakes.

What the examiner checks

Six criteria: Purpose, Content, Conciseness & Clarity, Genre & Style, Organisation & Layout, and Language.

Case notes: diabetes review for Mr Raymond Doyle

These case notes describe a fictional patient and were written for practice. Read them for five minutes and decide what the specialist nurse needs before you look at the task.

Patient details
  • Name: Mr Raymond Doyle
  • DOB: 11/02/1958 (68 y.o.)
  • Address: 14 Orchard Lane, Westbridge
  • Seen at Westbridge Health Centre for annual diabetes review: 21/10/2026
Social background
  • Widowed April 2026; lives alone; cooking for himself since
  • Daughter visits weekly; happy to attend appointments with him
  • Retired postman; enjoys gardening
  • Ex-smoker (stopped 2010); occasional beer
Medical history and medicines
  • T2DM (2016): metformin 1 g PO BD
  • Hypertension (2018): ramipril 5 mg OD
  • Cataract surgery R eye (2022)
  • NKDA
Nursing notes 21/10
  • HbA1c 75 mmol/mol (58 mmol/mol six months ago)
  • Reports increased thirst; diet irregular since wife's death (more takeaways and sweet snacks)
  • BP 136/82 mmHg; weight 92 kg (88 kg six months ago)
  • Foot check: reduced sensation both feet (monofilament); dry skin; small callus R heel; no ulcers
  • Often wears slippers outdoors as shoes feel tight
  • Pt keen to 'get back on track'
Plan
  • Refer to diabetes specialist nurse: treatment review and education (diet, foot care)
  • Referred to community podiatry service
  • Advised daily foot checks and well-fitting footwear
  • GP informed of HbA1c result

Writing task

You are the practice nurse at Westbridge Health Centre. Using the information in the case notes, write a letter of referral to Ms Angela Brooks, Diabetes Specialist Nurse, Diabetes Centre, Westbridge General Hospital, Hill Road, Westbridge.

  • Expand the relevant notes into complete sentences.
  • Do not use note form.
  • Use letter format.
  • The body of the letter should be approximately 180–200 words.

Model answer: nursing referral to a diabetes specialist nurse

Compare this model with your own letter. There is more than one way to score highly, but strong answers make the same selection decisions.

Ms Angela Brooks

Diabetes Specialist Nurse

Diabetes Centre

Westbridge General Hospital

Hill Road

Westbridge

21 October 2026

Dear Ms Brooks,

Re: Mr Raymond Doyle, DOB 11/02/1958

I am writing to refer Mr Doyle, aged 68, for a review of his type 2 diabetes, which has recently become poorly controlled, and for education on diet and foot care.

Mr Doyle was diagnosed in 2016 and takes metformin 1 g twice daily. At his annual review today, his HbA1c had risen from 58 to 75 mmol/mol over six months, and he reported increased thirst. Since his wife died in April, he has been cooking for himself and his diet has become irregular. He has also gained 4 kg.

On examination, monofilament testing showed reduced sensation in both feet. He has dry skin and a small callus on his right heel, but no ulcers. He often wears slippers outdoors because his shoes feel tight.

I would be grateful if you could review his treatment and provide education on diet and foot care. I have referred him to the community podiatry service and advised him to check his feet daily and wear well-fitting shoes.

Mr Doyle is motivated to improve his control. He lives alone, but his daughter visits weekly and is happy to attend appointments with him. Please contact me if you require further information.

Yours sincerely,

Practice Nurse

Westbridge Health Centre

Why this letter works

The model is mapped to the six criteria examiners use. According to OET, a Grade B letter typically scores 2 out of 3 for Purpose and 5 out of 7 for each of the other criteria. Selection is the skill behind most of them, so the lists below show which case notes were kept and which were left out.

Purpose

The first sentence asks for two things, a treatment review and education, and says why: his diabetes has become poorly controlled. The specialist nurse knows the request before reading any history.

Content

Every included note supports the referral: the rising HbA1c, thirst, weight gain, the diet change after his bereavement, the foot findings and what has already been done. Stable history such as the cataract surgery is left out.

Conciseness & Clarity

The review is summarised in five short paragraphs. Several foot findings become two clear sentences, and the diet notes become one cause-and-effect point.

Genre & Style

The tone is professional, nurse to specialist colleague. The request is polite and specific, using “I would be grateful if you could”, and the bereavement is mentioned briefly and respectfully.

Organisation & Layout

Purpose first, then diabetes control, then the foot findings, then the request and actions already taken, then social support. Standard letter layout with the recipient, date and Re: line.

Language

Accurate tenses (past simple for today's findings, present perfect for the diet change since April), precise results and doses copied from the notes, and no note-form abbreviations such as BD, OD or R.

Included because the reader needs it
  • Reason for referral: poor diabetes control and education needs
  • Metformin dose and year of diagnosis
  • HbA1c rise, thirst and weight gain
  • Irregular diet since his wife's death
  • Reduced sensation, dry skin and heel callus, with no ulcers
  • Slippers worn outdoors because shoes feel tight
  • Podiatry referral and advice already given
  • Motivation, living alone and daughter's support
Left out because it does not change the next step
  • Retired postman and gardening
  • Cataract surgery in 2022
  • Ex-smoker since 2010 and occasional beer
  • Hypertension, ramipril and blood pressure (stable, not the reason for referral)
  • Exact details of takeaways and snacks
  • GP informed of the result (internal, not for the specialist nurse)

Common mistakes in OET nursing referral letters

These errors cost marks in nursing referrals again and again, and each one is easy to fix once you can see it.

  • Opening with the patient's history instead of the reason for referral.
  • Asking vaguely for the patient to be “seen” without saying what you want the specialist to do.
  • Copying every reading from the notes, including stable blood pressure and old surgery.
  • Leaving out what you have already done, such as the podiatry referral and foot-care advice.
  • Describing the bereavement at length instead of linking it briefly to the diet change.
  • Using note-form abbreviations such as BD, T2DM or y.o. in the letter.
  • Forgetting the social support that will help the specialist plan appointments.

OET nursing referral letter FAQ

More exam questions are answered on the full OET FAQ page.

How long should an OET nursing referral letter be?

The body of the letter should be about 180–200 words. Writing a little more or less is not penalised on its own, but long letters usually include irrelevant case notes, which costs marks for content and conciseness.

What should the first paragraph of a nursing referral say?

Who the patient is, what you are asking the reader to do and why. The examiner looks for the purpose straight away, so name the request, such as a treatment review and education, in the opening paragraph.

Should I mention personal or social details in a referral letter?

Only when they affect the care the reader will give. Here the bereavement explains the diet change and the daughter's support helps with appointments, but his former job and hobbies do not change the next step.
