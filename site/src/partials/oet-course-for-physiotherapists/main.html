Who is this OET physiotherapy course for?

The course is built for physiotherapists who need OET Physiotherapy for registration abroad and want one clear route with real feedback.

Physiotherapists registering with the HCPC

The HCPC accepts OET Physiotherapy with a total of 1400 and no sub-test below 300, taken at a test centre. The UK pathway guide explains where OET fits in the HCPC international route.

Physiotherapists heading to Australia

All Ahpra National Boards, including the Physiotherapy Board of Australia, accept OET with new minimum scores for tests from 23 April 2026. The Australia pathway guide covers the APC route.

Physical therapists heading to the USA

CGFNS VisaScreen accepts OET for physical therapists, with new minimum scores for tests from 12 May 2026. The USA pathway guide explains state licensing and the English test choice.

How do you pass OET Writing as a physiotherapist?

OET Physiotherapy Writing gives you 45 minutes (5 minutes of reading time and 40 minutes of writing) to turn a set of case notes into one letter with a body of about 180–200 words. There is no automatic penalty for writing slightly over or under that range.

Discharge letters

Hand a patient over to a GP or community physiotherapist with their progress, current function, mobility aids and the home exercise programme they must continue.

Referral letters

Refer a patient to a GP, orthopaedic team or another professional, stating clearly why you are referring and what you are asking for.

Progress and update letters

Update a colleague on how a patient is responding to treatment and what should happen next.

What the examiner checks

Six criteria: purpose, content, conciseness and clarity, genre and style, organisation and layout, and language. A clear purpose in the opening line and a specific final request protect marks across several criteria.

The most common reason physiotherapists lose Writing marks is listing every treatment session instead of summarising the progress the reader needs. See a full worked example in the OET writing sample for physiotherapists, read the OET Writing tips, and use OET writing correction for extra letters.

What physiotherapy role-plays will you practise for OET Speaking?

OET Speaking takes about 20 minutes and has two role-plays. You get 3 minutes to prepare each card and about 5 minutes to complete it, with the interlocutor playing the patient, a relative or a carer.

Typical physiotherapy scenarios

  • Explaining a home exercise programme and why it matters
  • Responding to pain or fear of re-injury
  • Advising on a mobility aid or safe movement at home
  • Discussing return to work, sport or daily activities
  • Encouraging a patient who wants to stop their exercises

How you are assessed

Examiners score clinical communication (relationship building, understanding the patient’s perspective, providing structure, gathering information and giving information) as well as intelligibility, fluency, appropriate language and grammar. A calm structure and patient-friendly explanations matter more than advanced vocabulary.

The recorded course gives you 100+ Speaking cards across different scenarios, and the private 1:1 session lets you rehearse them with feedback. Practise with the physiotherapy role-play cards and read the OET Speaking tips before your session.

How do you prepare for OET Listening and Reading?

Listening and Reading are the same for every profession, so you sit exactly the same papers as nurses and doctors. What changes your score is method, not luck.

Listening (about 40 minutes, 42 questions)

Part A is note completion from two consultations, Part B is short workplace extracts and Part C is longer presentations or interviews. Train prediction before each extract and write exactly what you hear, with correct spelling. See the OET Listening tips.

Reading (60 minutes, 42 questions)

Part A is a strict 15-minute scanning task across four short texts; Parts B and C test detail, gist and inference. Use keyword anchors in Part A and eliminate distractors in Parts B and C. See the OET Reading tips.
