OET vs PTE: which English test should healthcare professionals take?

OET tests the English you use at work in healthcare, with a letter and role-plays written for your own profession. PTE Academic tests academic English in short, timed computer tasks. Ahpra accepts both, but none of the UK, Irish or New Zealand regulators we checked lists PTE Academic, so your regulator usually decides for you.

OET or PTE: the short answer

Start with your regulator, not the test. If its accepted tests are only OET and IELTS, as with the NMC, the GMC, NMBI and the New Zealand councils, you cannot plan your registration around a PTE Academic score, however high it is. If it accepts both, as Ahpra does, compare the formats below and choose the test that suits you. Scores and rules change, so confirm them on the regulator's own page before you book.

OET and PTE Academic side by side

Both tests cover listening, reading, writing and speaking, but the tasks, the timing and the way you sit them are very different.

Healthcare English or academic English?

OET designs Listening and Reading for all candidates on general health topics, while Writing and Speaking reflect workplace situations in your own profession. Pearson describes PTE Academic as a computer-based academic English test for study, migration or work abroad.

What OET rewards

Choosing the right case notes for a named reader, and a structured, patient-centred conversation. Speaking is marked on linguistic criteria and on clinical communication: relationship building, understanding and incorporating the patient's perspective, providing structure, information gathering and information giving.

What PTE Academic rewards

Speed and accuracy in short, timed items. The microphone opens on a countdown and each answer is recorded once; for Read Aloud, Pearson scores content, pronunciation and oral fluency. A fluent speaker who freezes on a timer can find this harder than a conversation.

How the scores work

Neither test has a single pass mark. Each regulator sets its own minimums, so the same result can be enough in one country and short in another.

OET: 0 to 500 for each sub-test

Scores go up in steps of 10, with grades from A (highest) to E (lowest). Grade B starts at 350 and C+ at 300. The GMC, the GPhC and the Irish medical and dental councils ask for B in each sub-test; the NMC, NMBI, the New Zealand medical and nursing councils and ECFMG accept 300 in Writing.

PTE Academic: 10 to 90

You receive an overall score and a score for each of speaking, writing, reading and listening. Of the regulators in this guide, only Ahpra publishes PTE Academic minimums for registration.

Worked example: checking two score reports against Ahpra

These are invented practice score reports written by OET with Dr Hesham to show the method, not real candidates or results. Both tests were taken after 23 April 2026.

Practice case 1: a physiotherapist with PTE Academic
  • Overall 70: meets the minimum of 63
  • Listening 68, Reading 72, Writing 75: all above 58, 59 and 60
  • Speaking 71: below the minimum of 76

Outcome: not enough from one sitting. A second PTE Academic sitting within 12 months could complete the requirement if it reaches 76 in Speaking and 63 overall, with no score in either sitting below 53, 54, 60 and 66. An OET result could not be added.

Practice case 2: a nurse with OET Nursing
  • Listening 380 and Writing 360: above 350
  • Speaking 390: above 360
  • Reading 350: grade B, but 10 points below Ahpra's 360

Outcome: short for Ahpra, yet the same report meets the NMC rule. A second OET sitting within 12 months with Reading at 360 or above, and no score in either sitting below Ahpra's floors, would meet the Australian rule.

Key takeaway

Read your regulator's table for your test date, line by line. A grade B is not always enough, a high overall PTE score cannot rescue one weak skill, and the gap tells you exactly which skill to train before a second sitting.

Who should choose OET, and who should choose PTE Academic?

Your regulator comes first, your strengths second, and convenience last.

UK, Ireland or New Zealand

Choose OET (or IELTS): none of the regulators we checked in these countries lists PTE Academic. UK dentists should read the GDC page first, because it names IELTS.

Australia

Both work. PTE Academic suits you if short computer tasks and a strict timer do not unsettle you and your speaking can reach 76. OET suits you if you communicate best in a clinical conversation and a structured letter.

Doctors heading to the USA

ECFMG's 2027 Pathways require OET Medicine from every applicant. See the USA pathway guide for the other steps.

You already hold a score

A recent PTE Academic result that meets Ahpra's table is a good reason not to switch. If your regulator does not list PTE, do not rely on it for registration.

Comparing OET with IELTS instead? Read OET vs IELTS. Dentists will find the dental rules and OET Dentistry tasks in the OET course for dentists.

Common mistakes when choosing between OET and PTE

Each of these can cost you a test fee and several weeks.

Treating a visa score as a registration score

A score that works for a visa or a university may not be on your regulator's list. Pearson says PTE Academic UKVI has the same content and scoring as PTE Academic; neither is on the NMC, GMC or HCPC list.

Mixing providers

A good OET Writing score and a good PTE Speaking score do not make a valid set. Ahpra states that results from different test providers cannot be combined.

Using the wrong Ahpra table

Tests taken on or before 22 April 2026 are assessed against Ahpra's older minimums; tests from 23 April 2026 against the new ones. Check the date on your report.

Booking the wrong version

PTE Academic cannot be taken at home, and OET@Home is refused by Ahpra, the HCPC, the GPhC and the Irish councils. Regulators also name the OET profession they want, such as Medicine for the GMC or Pharmacy for the GPhC.

OET vs PTE FAQ

Short answers for healthcare professionals choosing a test. More exam questions are on the full OET FAQ page.

Does the NMC accept PTE Academic?

No. The NMC says IELTS Academic and OET are the only English tests it can accept, and that it is not looking to accept any other tests at the moment.

What PTE Academic score does Ahpra require?

For tests taken on or after 23 April 2026, Ahpra asks for 63 overall, with at least 58 in Listening, 59 in Reading, 60 in Writing and 76 in Speaking, taken on computer at a test centre.

Is OET easier than PTE?

Neither test is easier for everyone. OET uses a letter from case notes and two role-plays with a person playing your patient; PTE Academic uses an essay and short spoken answers recorded on a computer. Choose the test your regulator accepts that suits your strengths.

Can I combine OET and PTE results?

No. Ahpra states that results from different test providers cannot be combined, and the NMC only combines two sittings of the same type of test.

Can I take PTE Academic or OET at home?

PTE Academic cannot be taken at home. OET@Home exists, but Ahpra, the HCPC, the GPhC, NMBI and the Medical Council of Ireland do not accept it, while the NMC and the GMC do.
