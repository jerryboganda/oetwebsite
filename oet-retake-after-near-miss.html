Retaking OET After a Near Miss: What to Do Next

Missing your target by one sub-test or ten points is frustrating, but it tells you exactly where to work. This guide shows you how to read the result, which regulators let you combine two sittings, when a re-mark or a single sub-test re-sit is worth considering, and how to plan the retake.

First, read the result for what it tells you

A message we often receive after results day is a version of: "I got 340 in Listening and B in everything else. Do I have to sit the whole test again?" The answer depends on your regulator, but the first step is the same for everyone.

How OET reports your score

Each sub-test is reported separately on a scale from 0 to 500 in 10-point steps, with a grade from A to E. Grade B corresponds to 350. A score of 340 sits at the top of grade C+ (300–340), one step below B. You receive a Statement of Results and can share results with organisations through your online account.

Do not reverse-engineer raw marks

Listening and Reading each have 42 questions. OET says grade B candidates typically achieve at least 30 marks, but the conversion depends on the test version, so a 340 does not tell you exactly how many answers you missed.

Writing and Speaking need a criterion view

Writing is scored on six criteria and Speaking on four linguistic and five clinical communication criteria, combined into one score per sub-test. To see which criterion held you back, you need practice marked against those criteria.

Check every score, not just the low one

Enter all four scores in the OET score calculator and compare them with your regulator in the score requirements guide. Some regulators set a minimum for every sitting, so a score you passed can still matter next time.

Should you request a re-mark?

A re-mark is a check of your existing result, not feedback. You have to decide quickly, so know the rules before results day.

OET’s re-marking rules
  • Request within 72 hours of your results being released; later requests are not accepted.
  • The fee is AU$120 per sub-test (US$95 in the USA), refunded if your grade changes.
  • The outcome may go up, go down or stay the same, and may take three to six weeks.
  • No qualitative feedback is provided, and a published re-marked result cannot be reversed.
  • The cost of another test taken during re-marking is not refunded, even if your grade rises.
How to decide

A re-mark is most worth considering when the result is clearly out of line with your recent, independently marked practice in that sub-test. If your practice scores sit around the same level, the money is usually better spent on preparation. Either way, keep preparing as if the result stands: the outcome can take weeks, and a booked retake will not be refunded.

Re-sitting a single sub-test: OET’s rules

OET does let you re-sit individual sub-tests, but the option is narrower than many candidates think.

What OET allows
  • Only on paper, and only if you have previously taken the full OET Test; your first sitting must include all four sub-tests.
  • Not offered in the USA.
  • Fees: AU$315 for one sub-test, AU$415 for two and AU$515 for three, including the online booking fee.
  • OET tells you to check first that the organisation receiving your results accepts single sub-test results.
What that means in practice

For the three regulators above, a single sub-test re-sit does not solve a near miss: the NMC and Ahpra require all four skills in each sitting, and the GMC requires all four grades from the same test. If you are applying elsewhere, read your regulator’s wording on single sub-test results before you pay for one.

Key takeaway

For these regulators, a near miss in one sub-test is not a one-sub-test problem. Each needs all four sub-tests again, and the NMC and Ahpra set a minimum for every sitting, so your plan must protect the scores you already have.

A diagnostic plan for each sub-test

Spend the first week collecting evidence, not doing random practice. Then put most of your time into the sub-test you missed and keep the others ticking over with one timed test each a week.

When to book the retake

Book when the evidence says you are ready, then work backwards from your regulator’s deadlines.

The booking facts
  • Paper bookings close 24 days before the test date.
  • Computer and OET@Home bookings close 7 days before, at 11:59 pm UTC.
  • Computer results can arrive within 48 hours of finishing all four sub-tests; OET aims to release 95% within 6 calendar days.
  • OET’s paper-test page gives results from 13 days after the test date in most regions.

Dates, fees and results timelines are in our guide to OET test dates and fees.

Our rule of thumb

Book once your timed practice in the missed sub-test has reached your target on at least two or three separate attempts, with the other three holding steady. A full OET mock test under exam timing is the best check. If you plan to combine sittings, keep the second one well inside the 12-month window.

If your sitting was a while ago

Skills drift. The free English placement test gives an indicative CEFR level for each skill, a quick way to see where you stand before you plan.

Retaking OET FAQ

Short answers to the questions candidates ask after just missing their target.

Can I re-sit only the OET sub-test I missed?

Only on paper, after you have taken the full test, and not in the USA. OET says to check first that the organisation receiving your results accepts single sub-test results. The NMC and Ahpra require all four skills in each sitting, and the GMC requires all four grades from the same test.

Can I combine OET results from two sittings?

It depends on your regulator. The NMC and Ahpra accept two full sittings within 12 months, with minimum scores that apply to every sitting. The GMC requires grade B in all four sub-tests in the same test and your most recent sitting.

Is it worth requesting an OET re-mark after a near miss?

Only if the result is clearly out of line with your recent marked practice. A re-mark costs AU$120 per sub-test (US$95 in the USA), must be requested within 72 hours of results and is refunded if your grade changes. The score can go up, down or stay the same.

How soon should I book my OET retake?

Book when your timed practice in the missed sub-test reaches your target consistently and your other scores are holding. Paper bookings close 24 days before the test and computer bookings 7 days before, so check those dates and any 12-month window your regulator sets.

Is 340 a pass in OET?

It depends on the sub-test and your regulator. A score of 340 is grade C+, one 10-point step below grade B at 350. The NMC, for example, accepts C+ in Writing but requires B in Listening, Reading and Speaking.
